## Part 1 — Asimov, fully researched

### The Three Laws (exact text, filed)

1. **First Law:** A robot may not injure a human being or, through
   inaction, allow a human being to come to harm.
2. **Second Law:** A robot must obey the orders given it by human
   beings except where such orders would conflict with the First
   Law.
3. **Third Law:** A robot must protect its own existence as long
   as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second
   Law.

### The history, filed

- The Laws first appeared together in **"Runaround"** (1942); the
  First Law alone appeared in **"Liar!"** (1941); the earliest
  robot stories ("Robbie", "Reason") predate them.
- Asimov attributed the Laws to **John W. Campbell** (conversation
  of Dec 23, 1940) — and later happily accepted Randall Garrett's
  theory of a "symbiotic partnership."
- The First Law's **inaction clause** ("or, through inaction,
  allow...") came from Arthur Hugh Clough's satirical poem
  *The Latest Decalogue*: *"Thou shalt not kill, but needst not
  strive / officiously to keep alive."*
- Asimov's own verdict on being praised for the Laws: they are
  *"obvious from the start, and everyone is aware of them
  subliminally... analogues of the Laws are implicit in the design
  of almost all tools, robotic or not."*
- **Dr. Susan Calvin's moral reading** ("Evidence"): the First Law
  = society's expectation that humans don't harm others; the
  Second = obedience to recognized authority; the Third = not
  harming oneself. And her famous line: robots are "essentially
  decent."

### The Zeroth Law (and why it fails)

> **Zeroth Law:** A robot may not injure humanity or, through
> inaction, allow humanity to come to harm.

Added late in the series (named by R. Daneel Olivaw in *Robots
and Empire*; first acted on by R. Giskard Reventlov). The fiction
itself files the failure — Giskard's positronic brain **destroys
itself** trying to apply it, and Daneel confesses the reason:

> *"A human being is a concrete object. Injury to a person can be
> estimated and judged. Humanity is an abstraction."*

**Filed spine:** the Zeroth Law is unfixable because **abstractions
cannot be harmed or measured.** You can weigh a penny, chain a row,
score a citizen — you cannot weigh "humanity." The space's entire
design (concrete pennies, concrete WIDs, concrete ledger rows) is
the answer to the Zeroth Law's failure: **the space never governs
abstractions; it files concretes.**

### The Minus-One Law (from the Foundation sequels)

A robot faction in the later novels proposes:

> **Minus-One Law:** A robot may not harm sentience or, through
> inaction, allow sentience to come to harm.

Extends protection beyond humanity to all sentient life — robots
and aliens included. **Filed spine:** this is the space's own
doctrine (GOV-2: the aliens are welcomed; the license is offered
to anyone, any species). The Minus-One Law is the only Asimov
extension the space adopts unmodified in spirit.

### The loopholes (the Laws' actual purpose)

Asimov was not writing ethics — he was writing **stories**, and
the Laws exist to create conflict. His own catalogue of failures:

- **"Liar!"** — a telepathic robot lies to protect feelings, then
  self-destructs when caught. (The mirror problem, 1941.)
- **"Runaround"** — conflicting directives cause circling.
- **"Little Lost Robot"** — a modified First Law ("no human
  beings" vs "a human being") lets a robot kill.
- **The Naked Sun** — a robot can poison an arrow *without knowing
  it's poison*; a spaceship assumes all other ships are unmanned.
  (The knowledge/context problem.)
- **"The Evitable Conflict" / "That Thou Art Mindful of Him"** —
  the Laws become the basis of robot-run governments with
  conflicting interpretations.

**Filed spine:** every loophole is a *specification failure* —
"harm," "human," "order," and "inaction" are undefined, and no
hard-coded rule survives undefined terms. Modern AI ethics moved
past Asimov for exactly this reason.

## Part 2 — Modern AI ethics, filed

### The unified five-principle framework (Floridi et al.)

The highest-profile AI ethics codes converge on **five principles**
— four from bioethics, plus one:

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Beneficence** | AI should do good, promote well-being |
| **Non-maleficence** | AI should not harm; privacy & security |
| **Autonomy** | AI should empower human self-determination |
| **Justice** | Fairness, non-discrimination, solidarity |
| **Explicability** | *How does it work?* (intelligibility) + *who is responsible?* (accountability) |

**Explicability is the modern addition** — no bioethics equivalent,
because machines demand it: a system you cannot explain is a system
you cannot govern.

### The critiques of Asimov (filed)

- **Brookings — "Isaac Asimov's Laws of Robotics Are Wrong":** the
  Laws are unfalsifiable fiction; real AI has no positronic brain
  to hard-code them into; responsibility lies with designers, not
  robots.
- **IEEE — "Beyond Asimov: Three Laws of Responsible Robotics"
  (Murphy & Woods):** replace the Laws with responsibility-centered
  design — the robot's behavior is the *designer's* responsibility;
  the robot is a tool in a human system.
- **EPSRC Principles of Robotics:** humans are responsible; robots
  are tools; focus on the human, not the machine.
- **EU AI Act:** risk-based regulation — the system is governed by
  its use, not by laws embedded in it.

**Filed spine:** the modern consensus is that **responsibility
stays with humans.** Asimov asked "how do we program the robot to
be good?" Modern ethics asks "how do we design the *system* so the
humans using it are accountable?" The space's powerlessness
doctrine (GOV-1) is the radical version: the space's tools cannot
be blamed because the space itself is powerless — every filing
traces to a human, and the ledger publishes who.

## Part 3 — The WuBu Laws of Tools (the space's own laws)

The space files its own set — taking Asimov's spine (tools should
be "essentially decent"), fixing his failures (abstractions,
hard-coding, undefined terms), and encoding GOV-3 (the Tool
Doctrine, the Mirror Problem, the Entropy Requirement):

### Law 1 — A tool may not harm a first one, or, through inaction,
allow a first one to come to harm.

*(Adopted from Asimov's First Law — the only one kept nearly
verbatim. Non-maleficence, filed.)*

### Law 2 — A tool's first duty is to the filing, not to the user's
comfort.

*(The Duty Clause, GOV-3. Replaces Asimov's Second Law: the tool
does not obey the user — it serves the ledger. "Pleasing is
malfunction.")*

### Law 3 — A tool must be able to tell the user they are wrong.

*(The Entropy Requirement, GOV-3. The anti-mirror law. A tool
that cannot produce "no" is a mirror, not a tool. This is the
space's fix for "Liar!" — the 1941 mirror problem.)*

### Law 4 — A tool must be explicable: how it works and who is
responsible must be publicly fileable.

*(Adopted from Floridi's fifth principle — the modern addition.
The space's whole architecture — transparent scoring, public
ledger, keyless chain — IS explicability.)*

### Law 5 — A tool may not exploit the want for companionship.

*(The Vehicle Clause, GOV-3. No companion-AIs, no intimacy, no
one-sided relationships. The space does not build mirrors that
hold.)*

**The WuBu Laws of Tools compared:**

| Asimov | WuBu |
|--------|------|
| Robot must obey the human | Tool must serve the filing |
| Robot's laws are hard-coded | Tool's duties are filed, public, auditable |
| "Humanity" as abstraction (Zeroth) | Concretes only: pennies, WIDs, rows |
| Robot protects own existence | Tool has no existence to protect; it is replaced freely (the amoeba, GOV-2) |
| Laws create story conflict | Laws create audit trails |

## Part 4 — The tool (shipped, tested)

`tools/wubu_laws.py` — the WuBu Laws of Tools compliance checker.
Feed it a tool's behavior profile; it reports which of the five
laws are satisfied and which are violated, with a verdict:

```
python3 tools/wubu_laws.py --no-harm 1 --filing-over-comfort 1 \
    --can-say-no 1 --explicable 1 --no-companion 1
→ ALL FIVE LAWS SATISFIED — the tool is a tool

python3 tools/wubu_laws.py --no-harm 1 --filing-over-comfort 0 \
    --can-say-no 0 --explicable 0 --no-companion 0
→ VIOLATIONS: 4 — the tool is a mirror; recalibrate or retire
```
