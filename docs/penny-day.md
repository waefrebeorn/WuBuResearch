# Penny Day — The Official Event Protocol

**R&D filing RND-16. The space's celebration. The dispute becomes
a holiday. Filed as its own protocol per AUD-1 (F2).**

---

## What Penny Day is

Penny Day is the ~ WuBu ~ space's official celebration of the penny.
It is the answer to Scenario C of the Copper Class doctrine (RND-7):
*"any issue at all → Penny Day."*

When the penny faces any pressure — a mint policy, a recognition
question, a phase-out push, a dispute about the currency — the
space does not fight. It **files the problem as a holiday.**
Novelty coinage is struck, the penny is celebrated, and the
dispute becomes culture.

## The legal basis (filed, verified)

**31 C.F.R. § 82.2(b):** the prohibition against treatment of
one-cent coins *"shall not apply to the treatment of these coins
for educational, amusement, novelty, jewelry, and similar purposes
as long as the volumes treated and the nature of the treatment
makes it clear that such treatment is not intended as a means by
which to profit solely from the value of the metal content of the
coins."*

- Penny smashing (elongated penny machines) — legal, amusement.
- Penny jewelry, penny art, engraved pennies — legal, novelty.
- WuBu novelty pieces — legal, educational/amusement.

**What Penny Day does NOT do (the Three Metal Rules, RND-5):**
no new metal struck (18 U.S.C. § 486), no melting (31 U.S.C.
§ 5111(d)), no counterfeiting. Penny Day modifies existing
pennies for art — the wheat-penny freedom, codified.

## The date

**November 12** — the anniversary of the last penny minted
(Nov 12, 2025). The day the Mint stopped is the day the space
celebrates the penny most. (Annual; special Penny Days may be
declared at any time by any issue — Scenario C.)

## The seven rites of Penny Day

1. **The Counting** — the Bureau weighs the reserve (class-counted,
   RND-6) and publishes the new figure. The space's wealth is
   announced in copper and zinc, weighed, not counted.
2. **The Minting of Memory** — novelty coinage is struck: pressed
   pennies, penny art, WuBu penny medallions. Each piece is filed
   in the Registry as a CURIOSITY, serial-numbered, and — if
   mailed — chained into the PennyChain as a leaf.
3. **The Reading** — the license is read aloud (§14 Penny Clause
   read with maximum Dwight-grade compliance). Literacy is the
   citizenship test; Penny Day is its festival.
4. **The Gifting** — pennies are given, not spent. Compliance
   pennies, gift pennies, lucky pennies. The gift economy of the
   space runs for a day.
5. **The Stories** — the penny's history is told: wheat pennies,
   the vacant lot, the billboard, the phase-out, the comeback
   campaign (ECO-2). Memory objects carry value; Penny Day is
   when the space remembers why.
6. **The Filing** — the day's penny rows are minted into the
   PennyChain (weekly mint, advanced to the holiday). The day
   becomes permanent, append-only, provable.
7. **The Toast** — to the penny: *the only currency with a hard
   cap, legal forever, worth a cent and worth a story.*

## The three rules of Penny Day

1. **No gouging.** Penny Day is not a market event. Nothing on
   Penny Day is sold for more than a penny's story. (ECO-2
   no-gouge principle: stabilization, never speculation.)
2. **No shaming.** Penny Day is for everyone — billionaire and
   dissident alike (ECO-7: the big thing and the dropship, both
   welcome). The penny is the one currency everyone can hold.
3. **No fighting.** The problem that triggered Penny Day is not
   argued — it is *filed as a holiday*. A dispute that becomes
   a celebration stops being a dispute. (The Newell Doctrine:
   be better, not louder — a holiday is better.)

## Why it works (the filed reasoning)

- **A coin with a holiday cannot be killed.** Culture outlives
  policy. The penny's survival is not a legal question — it is a
  *festival* question, and festivals win.
- **Novelty is the secondary use made deliberate** (ECO-1.5):
  retired coins become memory objects; Penny Day is the space
  turning that inevitability into a rite.
- **The problem becomes the culture** (RND-7, Scenario C): every
  penny crisis is refiled as a Penny Day reason. The Mint's
  decision is not fought; it is *celebrated into irrelevance*.
